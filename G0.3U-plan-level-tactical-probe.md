## 8. Verdict

**`PLAN_LEVEL_TACTICAL_PROBE = PREDICTIVE_BUT_TOO_EXPENSIVE`**

Grounds (spec §19):
- **The sidecar is predictive**: G4 causal pair is corrected deadline-OFF
  (BAD URPTDR → GOOD RRRR) with the pre-registered comparator and conservative
  override; G1 control does not invent escapes; G2/G5 never overridden; candidate
  recall PASS (RRRR at #5 of 6); enumeration itself is cheap (≤27ms/root).
- **But the cost explodes**: D/E on a single plan ranges 55ms (G4 RRRR) → 421ms
  (G4 production) → **44–80 SECONDS** (G1T1-class roots). At 500ms total budget,
  the tactical sidecar completes **0/10** and can never fix the BAD plan in
  production timing (0/10 not-BAD; 10/10 fallback = baseline BAD, soundly).
- The dominant cost is `catCaptureOrInterceptWithin4`: an EXACT_LOCAL cat BFS
  (legal-actions + push-box) per next-cat boundary; T1-class mouse turns produce
  11k–17k boundaries per candidate. 6 candidates ⇒ tens of thousands of cat BFSes.

Per §19, verdict stays PREDICTIVE_BUT_TOO_EXPENSIVE — no chance reduction, no
mouse-action deletion, no heuristic pruning, **no threshold tuning to make G4
fit** were performed (the comparator is pre-registered; the override is
conservative). This is a cost verdict, not a correctness verdict.

**Next direction (recorded, NOT implemented):** the one correct place to spend
the expensive D/E step is **once per candidate, ALREADY done here** — the cost
is intrinsic to the exact mouse-turn enumeration at T1 complexity. Any future
direction must either (a) cap the D/E to a bounded tactical window (which §19
forbids in this phase) or (b) find a sparse *plan-level* pre-filter that avoids
enumerating 300k-path mouse turns for uninteresting plans (cheap A/B/C already
exists — but the selector still had to evaluate it on every plan; 26-root
selector cost = 26×enum+cheap ≈ 5s total, well within budget; the expense is
solely the D/E per candidate).

No production changes. No commits.
